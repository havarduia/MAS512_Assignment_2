# Q4 · Toby in the toy city

**Autonomous navigation and longitudinal slip estimation in Webots**

Toby is a little two-wheeled robot living in a city made of coloured solids, in
*Wreck-It Ralph* style. With his camera he has to find the **red beacon**, hidden among the
café tables in the north-east corner of the square and invisible from the start. He has to
reach it without bumping into anything (statue, newsstand, stalls, tables, ice-cream cart,
planters, bins, fountain…) and **ignore the ten decoy beacons**, identical but in other
colours, including orange and magenta, the two closest to red. Meanwhile he records every
sensor and computes, at every step, the longitudinal slip of the wheels

```
s_long = (v_enc − v_x) / v_x        v_enc = r·(ω_L + ω_R)/2   (encoders)
                                     v_x   = true velocity     (Supervisor)
```

The same run is repeated on two squares that differ **only** in the wheel-floor friction:
wet (a) and dry (b).

## How to use it

1. Install [Webots R2025a](https://cyberbotics.com) and open `worlds/city_baseline.wbt`.
2. Press ▶: Toby sets off, and the top left corner shows the state, the slip and the ignored
   beacons. On arrival the simulation pauses and the data ends up in `data/baseline/`.
3. Repeat with `worlds/city_low_slip.wbt` (data in `data/low_slip/`).
4. Plots and videos, in `figures/`:

   ```
   pip install -r requirements.txt
   python analysis/plot_slip.py               # slip_map, slip_profile, speeds (item c)
   python analysis/camera_montage.py          # what_toby_sees
   python analysis/animate_run.py compare     # wet vs dry video (needs ffmpeg)
   ```

**All of it from one notebook.** `../MAS512_assignment2_gr1.ipynb` contains a single
cell that does steps 1 to 4 by itself: dependencies, world generation, the two
simulations headless (no GUI, full speed) and the figures, shown inline.

### Diagnostic scenarios

The generator also creates two dry worlds (`mu = 1.0`, hence no wet/dry comparison) to check
that the controller does not stay stuck indefinitely:

- `worlds/city_no_goal.wbt`: contains no red beacon at all. Toby alternates searching and
  exploring for eight cycles and then stops with `failure_reason = "goal_not_found"`
  (126.2 s in my run).
- `worlds/city_hidden_goal.wbt`: the red goal sits on the **west** side of the square. From
  the starting street the buildings cover it completely: Toby does his search rotation
  without seeing it, leaves the corridor and only then (t = 12.4 s, in the square) does the
  camera find it. Expected outcome: `goal_reached` in 22.1 s.

Both write their results into the respective folders `data/no_goal/` and
`data/hidden_goal/`. A diagnostic failure is recorded in `meta.json` and shown in the HUD; it
is never presented as `goal_reached`.

## Reference results

My two runs with Webots R2025a: same city, same robot, same code. Yours should come out the
same or nearly so:

| | wet square (a) | dry square (b) |
|---|---|---|
| wheel-floor friction | μ = 0.25, FDS = 0.03 | μ = 1.0, FDS = 0.0008 |
| red beacon found and reached | yes, in 46.8 s | yes, in 40.8 s |
| first search rotation (360° in place) | 10.2 s | 8.1 s |
| median longitudinal slip | **+7.1 %** | **−0.1 % (i.e. zero)** |
| distance measured by the encoders vs the true one | 28.7 m vs 26.8 m (+7.2 %) | 25.9 m vs 25.8 m (+0.5 %) |
| decoy colours seen and ignored | 7 out of 7 (10 beacons) | 7 out of 7 (10 beacons) |

The slip is computed over the states in which the robot is driving (`GOAL`, `RECALL`,
`WANDER`, `AVOID`) and with `|v_x| > 0.1 m/s`: the final braking, in which the wheels are
already stopped while the body is still sliding, would give a constant −100 % that says
nothing about the friction.

The goal is not visible from the start: Toby spins in place, drives towards the square,
glimpses the distant red among the tables and heads for it, going around the stalls and
recovering the direction with the IMU every time something covers it. On the wet floor the
wheels always turn a little faster than Toby actually advances; on the dry one the two
velocities coincide.
